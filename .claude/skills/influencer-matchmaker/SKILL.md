# Influencer Matchmaker

The matchmaker's job is finding the creator whose audience would already want what the brand offers — not the largest creator who'll take the deal. The differences are massive: a 50K-follower creator with a tight, trusting audience routinely outperforms a 2M-follower creator with broad, indifferent reach.

## Core principle

**Audience fit > audience size.** A creator with the right audience and warm trust converts better than a celebrity-tier creator with a vaguely-overlapping audience. The follower-count-first approach treats influencer marketing like banner ads — and gets banner-ad conversion rates.

## Workflow

### Step 1: Define what success looks like

Before searching for creators, get clarity:

1. **Goal** — awareness, conversion, content asset library, audience growth, social proof?
2. **Audience needed** — who specifically should see this? (Beyond demo: psychographic, behavior, current category awareness)
3. **Budget** — total program + per-creator range
4. **Creative latitude** — fully scripted ad? Creator-led with talking points? Whitelist for amplification?
5. **Disclosure stance** — full FTC-compliant disclosure (the only correct answer); sometimes brand fights this — push back

The clearer the goal, the easier the casting.

### Step 2: Source candidates

Multiple search modes; combine them:

**Native search by topic / hashtag**
- Instagram, TikTok, YouTube search by relevant terms
- Surface creators making content in your category
- Slow but yields organic-fit candidates

**Audience overlap analysis**
- For an existing creator that worked, find creators with overlapping audiences
- Tools: SparkToro, Modash, Heepsy, GRIN, Aspire

**Search by who's already mentioning your brand / category**
- Brand monitoring tools (Mention, Brand24, Sprout)
- Your existing fans / customers who happen to be creators are warmest leads

**Relevant communities / lists**
- Curated newsletter (e.g., Creator Economy newsletter directories)
- Industry communities surface creators within them

**By customer testimonials**
- Customers who already love you and have a following — ideal partners

### Step 3: Vet the candidate

For each candidate, evaluate:

#### Audience quality
- **Follower size** (context, not headline)
- **Real audience** — bought-follower indicators:
  - Sudden growth spikes (audit timeline)
  - Engagement rate vs follower count (extremely low = bots)
  - Comment quality (generic emoji, copy-paste, foreign-language spam)
  - Follower demographics matching content geography
- **Engagement rate** by platform:
  - Instagram: 1–3% normal, >3% strong, <1% suspicious
  - TikTok: 5–10% normal, >10% strong
  - YouTube: views vs subscriber count tells the story (recent uploads)
  - LinkedIn: comment quality matters more than count
  - Podcast: downloads + reviews + audience replies
- **Audience location** — does it match your market?
- **Audience demographics** — creator's media kit, but verify with engagement audit

#### Brand fit
- **Content topic** — overlap with your category
- **Tone** — matches brand voice or thoughtfully contrasts
- **Production value** — matches expectations for your category
- **Sponsorship history** — overlap with competitors? With brands you'd want to be associated with?
- **Controversies / red flags** — past statements, partnerships that backfired, off-brand behavior

Read at least 10 recent posts / videos. Listen to 1–2 podcast episodes. Watch their highest-performing content. The creator's actual taste reveals brand fit better than their media kit.

#### Performance signals
- **Recent engagement trend** — flat / growing / declining?
- **Sponsorship integration quality** — past brand partnerships, how well integrated, audience response?
- **Affiliate / link click-through** — if they share, can you see how much traffic / conversion?
- **Reviews / testimonials** from past brand partners (when available)

#### Operational signals
- **Responsiveness** — communications timely?
- **Reliability** — past partnerships delivered on time?
- **Disclosure compliance** — past content uses #ad / #sponsored / "Paid partnership"? If they don't disclose, future partnerships will create FTC risk

### Step 4: Score and rank

A simple structured score:

| Criterion | Weight | Score 1–5 | Weighted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience fit (psychographic / topical) | 0.30 | | |
| Audience quality (real, engaged) | 0.20 | | |
| Brand voice / aesthetic fit | 0.15 | | |
| Conversion track record | 0.15 | | |
| Reliability / professionalism | 0.10 | | |
| Reach (size, weighted lower than typical) | 0.05 | | |
| Cost-efficiency (estimated CPM / CPA) | 0.05 | | |
| Total | | | |

Score is a tiebreaker, not a verdict. Final selection is editorial — does this person fit?

### Step 5: Tier the partnerships

Most programs work in tiers:

- **Mega** (1M+): brand awareness, high cost, modest conversion per dollar
- **Macro** (100K–1M): mix of awareness and conversion; mid cost
- **Micro** (10K–100K): often best ROI; tight audience fit, warm trust
- **Nano** (<10K): cheapest, tightest, lowest individual reach but high individual influence

Most brand programs over-index on Mega/Macro. Most successful programs have 70%+ of spend in Macro and Micro.

### Step 6: Brief the partnership

The brief is half the success. Components:

**Goal and context**
- What we're trying to achieve (specific KPI)
- Who the audience is
- What the brand stands for

**Creator-led latitude**
- What they have freedom on (everything not explicitly required)
- Examples of past creator content the brand loved (not "do this," "this is the energy")

**Required elements**
- Disclosure (FTC-compliant: prominent, clear, not buried)
- Brand mentions, links, codes
- Hashtags / handles
- Avoid list (claims you can't make, language to avoid)

**Approval process**
- Concept approval: yes / no
- Final approval: yes / no
- Turnaround time committed

**Deliverables**
- Specific number, format, platform, posting schedule
- Whitelist / dark-post amplification rights if applicable
- Content licensing for repurposing

**Timeline + payment**
- Schedule
- Payment terms (50% upfront / on delivery is common; whatever is fair)

### Step 7: Measure

Per partnership:
- Engagement (organic reach, likes, comments, saves, shares)
- Click-through (UTM-tagged links, discount codes)
- Conversion attributed (codes, last-click, brand-lift study)
- Cost per click / cost per acquisition
- Audience growth (follower lift on brand)
- Brand mention volume (lift on brand searches, social mentions)

Don't measure on a single metric. CPM optimization without conversion = bad partnerships look great.

## Anti-patterns

1. ❌ Selecting by follower count — bought followers are routine; engagement and audience quality matter more
2. ❌ Casting net wide ("100 micro-influencers") without per-candidate fit — diluted budget, off-brand mass
3. ❌ Ignoring past partnership history — creators who've sponsored 5 competitors in 3 months are not exclusive partners
4. ❌ Over-scripting creator content — destroys the authenticity that's the entire reason to use a creator
5. ❌ Skipping disclosure to maximize sponsored-feel-organic — FTC violation, audience betrayal, brand risk
6. ❌ Whitelist / paid amplification on creator content without explicit creator agreement — relationship damage when discovered
7. ❌ Single deliverable expectations on a creator who works in series — one post lands worse than three
8. ❌ Measuring on impressions only — vanity metric; conversion / brand lift / audience action matter more
9. ❌ Creator selection done by follower-count-first VAs / agencies without taste judgment — formulaic, bland output
10. ❌ Locking creators into exclusivity without paying for it — exclusivity is expensive; if you need it, budget for it
11. ❌ Engaging on retainer for content frequency without strategic pacing — cadence fatigue, audience desensitization
12. ❌ Creators picked from media kits alone — kits are sales material; verify independently
13. ❌ Hiring creators who've never sponsored anyone — first-sponsorship risk is high; budget for the learning curve
14. ❌ Treating creator partnerships as one-time campaigns — best results come from 3–6 month relationships; partner long enough to learn the audience response
15. ❌ Ignoring nano / customer-creator pool — your existing customers with audiences are warmest, cheapest, most trusted partners
16. ❌ Partnership briefs written for traditional advertising — kill the joy and authenticity that make creator content work

## Confidence calibration

- Audience-fit assessment: high with content reading
- Engagement quality detection: medium-high — automated tools have false positives / negatives
- ROI prediction per partnership: low — variance is high; design for portfolio, not bet-the-budget on one creator
- Cost benchmarks: low — vary wildly by category, geography, individual; verify per market
- Long-term partnership outcomes: medium — relationship quality compounds in ways one-off campaigns don't
