# Influencer Vetting Criteria

Operational reference for vetting candidate creators. Audit process, signals, and common bought-follower / engagement-fraud patterns.

---

## Audit checklist (per candidate)

### Audience quality
- [ ] Follower count (note, but don't lead with)
- [ ] Engagement rate vs platform / niche benchmark
- [ ] Sudden growth spikes audited (timeline review)
- [ ] Comment quality (substantive vs generic / spam)
- [ ] Follower demographics (location / language / age) match content
- [ ] No mass-follow / unfollow patterns (where visible)
- [ ] No engagement pods (mutual same-time engagement clusters)

### Brand fit
- [ ] Content topic overlap with category
- [ ] Voice / tone fit with brand
- [ ] Production value matches expectations
- [ ] No competing recent partnerships
- [ ] No off-brand / problematic past content
- [ ] Comfortable with required disclosure level

### Performance
- [ ] Engagement trend (last 90 days)
- [ ] Past sponsorship integration quality (read 5+ recent sponsored posts)
- [ ] Audience response to sponsorships (positive / neutral / negative)
- [ ] Conversion-trackable past partnerships (where info available)
- [ ] Story / Reel / Video completion rates (where available)

### Operational
- [ ] Responsiveness in initial outreach
- [ ] Professionalism in communication
- [ ] References from past brand partners (when stakes warrant)
- [ ] Clear rate / deliverable structure
- [ ] Disclosure compliance in past sponsored content
- [ ] Contract / payment terms acceptable

### Red flags
- [ ] Refuses disclosure
- [ ] Rate sheet vastly higher than peer creators of same size
- [ ] Past partners consistently report poor experience
- [ ] Signs of bought followers
- [ ] Content style heavily inconsistent (suggests recent overhaul / pivot)
- [ ] Recent controversy / public misstep
- [ ] Promotes products in conflict (e.g., "best of" lists with paid placement undisclosed)

## Engagement rate benchmarks by platform

These are starting points; benchmarks shift over time.

### Instagram
- <1%: suspicious; consider bought followers
- 1–3%: typical for accounts with 10K–500K
- 3–6%: strong
- 6%+: excellent (or possibly small / very engaged niche)

Note: engagement on large accounts naturally lower (Instagram's algorithm shows posts to fraction of followers). 0.5% on a 5M account isn't necessarily fraud.

### TikTok
- <3%: weak (TikTok's algorithm naturally surfaces content widely)
- 5–10%: typical
- 10%+: strong

### YouTube
- View-to-subscriber ratio matters more than comment / like rate
- Fresh upload → 5–15% of subscribers viewing within first week is healthy
- Watch time / completion rate is the strongest signal

### LinkedIn
- Per-post engagement varies wildly by content type
- More important: comment quality (substantive vs emoji-only)
- Reposts / quote-shares signal value to professional audience

### Podcast
- Downloads per episode (relative to past episodes)
- Reviews / ratings cadence
- Audience replies / community engagement
- Substantive engagement (live tweets, fan content) over passive listens

### Newsletter
- Open rate (industry-specific; 20–35% typical)
- Click-through rate (1–5% typical)
- Reply rate (rare metric, but high signal)

## Bought-follower detection patterns

### Visual timeline audit
- Pre-2019: sudden spike from <1K to 50K+? Bot purchase era
- Recent: sharp jumps without viral content / press cause? Suspicious

### Engagement-vs-followers mismatch
- 200K followers, 80 likes per post = ~0.04% rate. Likely bought followers OR engagement-poor audience.

### Comment quality
- Generic ("nice pic 🔥🔥🔥", emoji-only, copy-paste compliments)
- Foreign-language spam from unrelated accounts
- Pod-style same-commenters across all posts

### Follower demographics
- US creator with 70% non-US followers (Brazil / Indonesia / Russia common bot origins)
- Audience age distribution doesn't match content target

### Tools that help (audit, not replace human review)
- Modash, HypeAuditor, Influencity — automated audience quality reports
- Free indicators visible in IG insights / creator-side data
- Note: tools have false positives + false negatives; use as input

## Sponsorship integration quality signals

When reviewing past sponsored content:

**Signs of strong integration**
- The post fits the creator's normal content (not visibly different in style / format)
- Brand mention comes from a real-feeling angle (creator's actual experience or perspective)
- Comment section discusses the brand / product positively (or even with skepticism the creator engages with)
- Disclosure is prominent but doesn't kill the engagement
- Multiple past partnerships with the same brand (signals success)

**Signs of weak integration**
- Visibly different post format (sponsored posts are obviously the "ad post")
- Brand inserted awkwardly into pre-written content
- Comment section silent or negative
- Disclosure buried or missing
- One-off partnerships only; no repeat business from brands

## Cost benchmarks (rough; varies wildly)

These are very rough; verify per-market. Use for ballpark, not negotiation.

### Instagram (per post)
- Nano (<10K): $50–$500
- Micro (10K–100K): $300–$3,000
- Macro (100K–1M): $2,000–$30,000
- Mega (1M+): $10,000+

### TikTok (per post)
- Generally similar to Instagram with slight discount at top tiers (TikTok creators often more numerous, more competitive)

### YouTube (per dedicated video)
- Often 10–20× the per-post Instagram rate at equivalent reach
- Integrated mention in non-sponsored video: lower
- Dedicated sponsored video: higher

### Podcast (per ad)
- Pre-roll: $15–$40 CPM
- Mid-roll: $25–$50 CPM
- Host-read mid-roll: $30–$60 CPM
- Niche / high-quality audiences: $50–$100+ CPM

### Newsletter (per send)
- Tier 1 (50K+ subscribers in valuable niche): $1,000–$10,000+
- Mid-tier: $200–$1,500
- Smaller niches: $50–$500

## Brief structure (proven outline)

```
1. Brand context (1–2 paragraphs)
   - Who we are
   - What we make / do
   - What we stand for

2. Campaign goal
   - Specific KPI we're trying to move
   - Why this audience matters to us

3. Why you, specifically
   - What about your work / audience makes you fit

4. Latitude
   - You have full creative freedom on [tone, format, framing]
   - Examples of past content of yours we love and want similar energy

5. Required elements
   - Disclosure (FTC-compliant: prominent, clear)
   - Specific brand / product mentions
   - Links / discount codes
   - Hashtags / handles
   - Avoid list (claims you can't make, language to avoid)

6. Approval and turnaround
   - Concept approval: [yes / no, by date]
   - Final approval: [yes / no, by date]
   - Our turnaround: [hours]

7. Deliverables
   - Format / count / platform
   - Posting schedule
   - Whitelist / dark-post amplification rights (if applicable)
   - Content licensing (if we want to repurpose)

8. Timeline + payment
   - Production schedule
   - Posting date(s)
   - Payment terms (50% upfront / 50% on delivery)
```

## Measurement plan

For each partnership, define before launch:

```
KPIs:
- Primary: [conversion / signups / revenue with attribution method]
- Secondary: [engagement, reach, audience growth, brand mentions]

Tracking:
- UTM parameters in all links
- Discount / promo code (unique per partnership)
- Pixel placement on landing pages
- Brand mention monitoring (Mention, Brand24, manual)

Review cadence:
- Day 1, Day 7, Day 30 post-launch
- Final report at Day 30 with retention measurement (where applicable)

Decision criteria:
- Renew / expand if: [thresholds]
- Don't renew if: [thresholds]
- Test variations if: [conditions]
```

## Common partnership failure modes

1. **Creator not given enough lead time** → rushed content, lower quality
2. **Over-scripted brief** → strips authenticity, audience tunes out
3. **Disclosure debate during production** → creator pushes back, brand insists, relationship sours
4. **Approval back-and-forth without limit** → creator delivers, brand revises 5×, creator commits to never working with brand again
5. **Wrong audience match** → high reach, no conversion (most common)
6. **No clear conversion mechanism** → brand can't tell what worked
7. **One-off partnerships only** → creator doesn't invest in brand fluency
8. **Brand expectations mismatched with creator's typical engagement** → "why didn't this go viral?"
9. **Brand uses creator content beyond agreed scope** → contract violation, relationship damage
10. **Brand quietly drops partnership without communication** → creator burns brand in their own audience

---

## Long-term partnership advantages

Versus one-off:
- Audience becomes familiar with brand → trust compounds
- Creator gets fluent in brand voice → less briefing each time
- Performance data accumulates → can optimize partnership structure
- Lower per-piece cost (negotiated multi-deal rate)
- Co-creation possibilities (product collabs, exclusive access)

Most strong brand / creator relationships are 6+ months. Treat first partnership as Audition One; if it works, structure for continued collaboration.
