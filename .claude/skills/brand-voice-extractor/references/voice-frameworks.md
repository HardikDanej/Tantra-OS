# Voice Extraction Frameworks

Reference frameworks used in voice work. Use as inputs to the extraction process — none is "the answer."

---

## Nielsen Norman tone-of-voice dimensions

NN/g identifies four tone dimensions, each on a continuum:

| Dimension | Pole 1 | Pole 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Funny vs serious | Funny | Serious |
| Formal vs casual | Formal | Casual |
| Respectful vs irreverent | Respectful | Irreverent |
| Enthusiastic vs matter-of-fact | Enthusiastic | Matter-of-fact |

A brand sits somewhere on each axis. Different content / contexts within a brand may shift along these axes (e.g., crisis comms more serious; product launch more enthusiastic) but the brand's **center of gravity** on each axis is consistent.

For voice extraction: place the brand on each axis, with example sentences from the corpus that anchor the position.

---

## Brand voice quadrants (various agency frameworks)

Most agency-style voice frameworks reduce to a 2×2 with axes like:

| | Casual | Formal |
|---|---|---|
| **Authoritative** | Confident insider | Expert authority |
| **Approachable** | Friendly peer | Helpful guide |

The brand's quadrant suggests the dominant register. But the framework risks oversimplifying — many strong brands span quadrants intentionally.

---

## The voice prism (Mailchimp-style)

Mailchimp's classic brand voice document used a prism of qualities, each with explicit do / don't:

| Quality | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Plainspoken | Use accessible words | Use jargon |
| Genuine | Match emotional register | Be cloying or performative |
| Translator | Make complex things clear | Be condescending |
| Familiar | Sound like a person | Be informal to a fault |

The do / don't structure is highly actionable for new writers. Adapt to the brand's own qualities (3–5 qualities is the sweet spot).

---

## Characteristic + opposite

A useful exercise:

For each voice quality the brand wants, name its opposite:
- "Direct" → not vague, not roundabout, not hedge-y
- "Witty" → not po-faced, not earnest-only, not pun-heavy
- "Warm" → not cold, not corporate, not gushing

The opposite is a guardrail. "Direct, but not blunt" is more useful than just "direct."

---

## Persona-as-voice

Some brands describe voice through a persona: "We sound like a [archetype]."

- Mailchimp: like a smart friend who happens to know about email marketing
- Slack: like a polite, helpful colleague
- Wendy's (social): a snarky teen
- Patagonia: a thoughtful environmentalist

Persona-as-voice is good shorthand for new contributors. Risk: literalism — the persona is a metaphor, not a script.

## Brand archetypes (Jung-derived, Mark & Pearson)

12 archetypes brands can align with:

| Archetype | Core motivation | Example brand |
|---|---|---|
| Innocent | Safety / happiness | Coca-Cola |
| Sage | Truth / wisdom | Google (early), TED |
| Explorer | Freedom / discovery | Patagonia, Jeep |
| Outlaw | Rebellion / liberation | Harley-Davidson |
| Magician | Transformation | Disney, Apple |
| Hero | Mastery / courage | Nike |
| Lover | Intimacy / pleasure | Chanel, Häagen-Dazs |
| Jester | Joy / play | Old Spice, Skittles |
| Everyman | Belonging | IKEA, Levi's |
| Caregiver | Service | Volvo, Johnson & Johnson |
| Ruler | Control / order | Mercedes, Rolex |
| Creator | Innovation | Lego, Adobe |

Archetype-based voice work is heavyweight; useful for brand-building but can feel forced if applied without conviction.

## Voice-vs-tone

A common distinction:
- **Voice**: the consistent personality of the brand
- **Tone**: the specific emotional register for the moment / context

Same voice; different tone for celebration vs apology vs crisis.

Voice extraction = stable patterns. Tone extraction = situational variations.

A complete voice document covers both: who the brand is consistently, AND how the voice modulates in specific contexts (sales / support / crisis / celebration / apology).

## Sentence-level voice DNA

Beyond frameworks, voice lives at the sentence level. Useful axes to characterize:

### Sentence length and rhythm
- Average length
- Distribution (mostly short / mostly long / mixed)
- Pattern (rhythmic / staccato / flowing)
- Use of fragments

### Vocabulary
- Reading level (Flesch-Kincaid, Hemingway score)
- Specific words used disproportionately
- Specific words avoided
- Jargon density and which jargon

### Punctuation and formatting
- Em dashes, semicolons, parentheticals
- Lists, bullets, headings
- ALL CAPS, italics, bold for emphasis
- Emoji (which, how often, in what register)

### Tonal moves
- Humor (deadpan / observational / self-deprecating / absurd / none)
- Vulnerability (admits / hedges / never)
- Stance (takes positions / observes / hedges)
- Direct address ("you" frequency, "we" frequency)

### What's NOT done
- Topics avoided
- Tonal registers absent
- Formats avoided
- Words explicitly off-limits

The sentence-level patterns are what make voice operational; frameworks are what make voice understandable.

## Combining frameworks

A complete voice document might include:

1. **One-line essence** (informal positioning)
2. **NN/g axes** (4 dimensions, with brand position on each)
3. **3–5 voice qualities with do/don't** (Mailchimp-style)
4. **Persona** (optional — useful for new contributors)
5. **Archetype alignment** (optional — for brand-building level work)
6. **Sentence-level DNA** (operational specifics)
7. **Tone modulation** (how voice shifts by context)
8. **Examples** (from corpus, the load-bearing element)

Don't use all of these — pick 3–4 that fit the brand's complexity and the team's needs.

---

## What frameworks don't replace

- **Reading the corpus**. Frameworks structure the analysis; they don't substitute for the analysis.
- **Specifics**. A framework says "casual"; the brand voice work has to specify what casual looks like in this brand's words.
- **The "what's not said" section**. Frameworks rarely capture this; the corpus does.
- **Operational rigor**. A framework is a thinking tool; the voice document needs to be a working tool with examples and rules a writer can apply.

The framework is scaffolding. The voice document, with its examples and specifics, is the building.
