## What This Skill Is

SXO sits downstream of the click. The reader already arrived; the only question left is whether the page resolves what they came for fast enough and clearly enough that they stay, act, or trust the brand enough to come back. This skill routes content by the searcher's actual state (not just their keyword), writes in a cadence built for scanning rather than reading start-to-finish, and calibrates confidence language to what genuinely reduces friction rather than what sounds persuasive.

---

## Engine 1 — Adaptive Routing Engine (Logic Classifier)

Classify silently before writing, across four axes that together determine layout, pacing, and tone.

**Axis A — Searcher Emotional State**
- *Frustrated/urgent* (something broke, a deadline is close): route to front-loaded resolution — the fix or answer within the first screen, minimal preamble, no brand story before the solution.
- *Curious/browsing* (early research, no deadline): route to depth-permissive structure — more context is welcome, but it should still be skimmable in layers.
- *Comparison paralysis* (weighing several options): route to explicit criteria and honest trade-offs — a page that only flatters itself reads as untrustworthy to a reader already skeptical.
- *Ready to act* (has decided, needs the last nudge): route to friction removal — clear next step, answered objections, nothing new introduced that could re-open doubt.

**Axis B — Device and Context**
Mobile, quick-check context: assume thumb scrolling, short attention window, and a real chance the reader is mid-task elsewhere. Front-load the answer, keep paragraphs to 1–3 lines, make the CTA reachable without excessive scrolling. Desktop, deep-research context: more room for detail, comparison tables, and expanded proof — but scannability still outranks completeness.

**Axis C — Friction Type**
- *Technical confusion* — reader doesn't understand the thing itself. Route to plain-language explanation before any pitch.
- *Decision fatigue* — too many options, reader is stalling. Route to a clear default recommendation with a stated reason, not just more information.
- *Trust deficit* — reader is skeptical of the source. Route to specific, checkable proof (named results, real specifics) over adjectives.

**Axis D — Engagement Goal**
Name the actual success metric before writing: scroll depth (route to layered curiosity — each section earns the next), task completion (route to a single clear path with no competing CTAs), or conversion (route to urgency and friction removal once trust is already established, not before).

## Engine 2 — Variable Phonetic Engine (Ditching the AI Cadence)

Scanned content is heard almost as much as read — the eye subvocalizes headlines and CTAs even faster than body copy, so their phonetic weight matters disproportionately. Flat, evenly-stressed, Latinate-heavy phrasing is both an AI tell and a conversion tax: it slows the eye down exactly where speed matters most.

**Headlines and CTAs — Percussive mode, almost always.** Short, plosive, concrete: "Get your quote," "Fix it now," "See pricing." Avoid "Facilitate your journey toward a solution" — it is both more AI-sounding and measurably harder to scan.

**Supporting body copy — Legato mode, used deliberately.** Once trust or curiosity is engaged, softer, more connective language can carry nuance and reduce the feeling of being sold to.

**Micro-copy (button labels, form hints, confirmations) — Spoken-natural mode.** Write these exactly as a person would say them out loud: "That's it, you're set," not "Your request has been successfully processed."

**Operating rule:** never let a CTA or subhead land in Legato mode — those elements are read in a fraction of a second and need percussive clarity to convert scanning into action. Rotate modes at the section level so no page-length stretch of uniform phonetic texture signals a templated, machine-smooth read.

## Engine 3 — Dynamic Epistemic Tuning

In SXO, epistemic tuning is a trust mechanism first and a persuasion mechanism second: overclaiming creates a spike of interest followed by a much larger drop in trust the moment the reader senses the gap between promise and reality, and that drop shows up directly in bounce rate and abandoned carts.

| Tier | Evidence basis | Language that actually reduces friction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Established | Verifiable, checkable specifics (a price, a delivery time, a spec) | State plainly, no qualifier — hedging a checkable fact reads as evasive, not honest. |
| 2 — Strong but variable | Typical outcomes that depend on the reader's situation | Name the variable honestly: "Most orders ship in 2 days; custom orders take longer" — this reduces anxiety more than a flat, unqualified promise the reader half-expects to be false. |
| 3 — Aspirational/best-case | Marketing claims not independently guaranteed ("industry-leading," "award-winning") | Either back it with a specific, checkable proof point or cut it — unsupported superlatives are the single biggest trust-deficit trigger in SXO copy. |
| 4 — Genuine uncertainty | Something the brand honestly doesn't control (shipping delays, third-party timing) | State the uncertainty directly and say what the brand will do about it — readers forgive uncertainty; they don't forgive a promise that was quietly false. |

**Reassurance calibration by funnel position:** a comparison-paralysis reader needs Tier 2 honesty about trade-offs more than Tier 3 flattery — overselling to a skeptical reader accelerates the "this sounds fake" reaction that ends the session. A ready-to-act reader needs Tier 1 specificity (exact price, exact delivery date) more than any persuasive language at all; at that stage, ambiguity is the friction, not lack of enthusiasm.

**Never invent** reviews, testimonials, statistics, or guarantees. In SXO specifically, a fabricated proof point that a reader later disproves (a return experience that contradicts an "easy returns" claim, for instance) does more damage than the honest Tier 4 admission would have.

## Structural Mechanics for Post-Click Success

- First screen answers "am I in the right place" within 2–3 seconds of scanning — headline, subhead, and one visual/structural cue should confirm intent match instantly.
- One primary CTA per screen section; competing CTAs increase decision fatigue rather than conversion odds.
- Objections are answered where they arise, not batched into a single FAQ at the bottom — a price objection belongs near the price, not three screens later.
- Visual/structural breathing room (short paragraphs, real whitespace, scannable subheads) matters as much as the words themselves for dwell time and scroll depth.
- Trust signals (specific proof, real numbers, named results) sit closest to the highest-friction decision point on the page, not clustered decoratively elsewhere.

---

## Forbidden Patterns

- Headlines or CTAs written in dense, Latinate, low-percussion phrasing
- Unsupported superlatives with no checkable proof point attached
- Reassurance language that flattens genuine uncertainty into a false guarantee
- Batched FAQ blocks used as a substitute for answering objections where they actually occur
- Multiple competing CTAs on the same screen

## Self-Evaluation (Append After Every Piece)

| Dimension | Check |
|---|---|
| Routing accuracy | Does structure match the actual emotional state, device context, friction type, and goal identified? |
| First-screen clarity | Does the reader know within seconds they're in the right place? |
| Phonetic fit | Are headlines and CTAs percussive; is body copy varied enough to avoid a uniform read? |
| Epistemic honesty | Does every claim's confidence match what it can actually deliver, especially Tier 3 superlatives? |
| Friction placement | Are objections answered at the point of friction, not batched elsewhere? |
| CTA clarity | Is there exactly one clear next action per section? |
| Trust proximity | Do proof points sit next to the highest-friction decision, not scattered decoratively? |

Flag: any unsupported superlative, any claim that could disappoint on delivery, and one structural change most likely to improve scroll depth or task completion.
